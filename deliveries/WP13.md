## What was done (做了什么)

### (a) Where construction time goes

Capability: per-stage and per-trajectory time accounting of F2 agent construction from raw ledgers, receipts and session files; Template-vs-Text comparison. Real-run evidence: the F2 runs themselves (GB10 worker, Codex `gpt-6-astra`, see [L]); this WP added no new run.

**Within-session split is not measurable.** The F2 author sessions ran as `codex exec --ephemeral` (wrapper `F2/formal-v7/<stage>/codex-isolated`), and `provider.stdout` events carry no timestamps. Time inside a session therefore cannot be divided into reading context, writing code and debugging. What is measurable: total author wall per session, provider reconnect stalls (stderr timestamps), and the composition of actions (commands, bytes, tokens). The sessions ran on the macOS control host (workspace under `/private/tmp/agentic-f2-authors-20261027/`, see `provider-command.json`) without a GPU; solving (spark) and compile and measurement (GB10 worker) happen after each session, as separate stages.

### (b) Prediction error of the existing GB10 models

Capability: pairing of every frozen F2 latency model (author-written analytic formulation over the shared GB10 `hardware.json` [H]) with the GB10 measurements of the points it was asked about, plus ranking and regret statistics. Real-run evidence: the F2 GB10 batches ([F], `F2/formal-v7/feedback-*/environment.json` reports `"gpu": "NVIDIA GB10"`).

Per v7 stage: 1 model point, 1 family default and 4 author-chosen probes are measured (540 configs over 90 stages). 43 failed qualification and have no latency; 6 lack a prediction (one stage without a CPU formula audit). After deduplicating equal assignments: 491 pairs. Error = (predicted − measured) / measured. "Unseen" excludes assignments measured in an earlier stage of the same trajectory, whose feedback the author had. Ordering accuracy counts only measured pairs that differ by more than 3% (D78), about 3× the median capture noise.

Reading B1–B5:

- Before any feedback on the task (fresh round 1), the models over-predict by a median 16.9% and only 38.1% of points fall within 10%. After one or more feedback rounds the median error drops to 4.1% (4.6% on unseen points), but the tail stays wide (p90 49.8%).
- Ranking is weak. On measured pairs that differ by more than 3%, the model orders 67.6% correctly overall and 55.9% in round 1. The frozen model point is the measured best of its stage in 25.0% of stages. Its regret against the best measured point is small (median 1.5%, p90 10.1%), because the measured set clusters around the model point.
- attn-medium is hardest: median error 11.1%, and the final recommendations under-predict by a median 14.5%. Its K+V working set is 1.0002× `hw_l2_bytes`, i.e. at the L2 capacity boundary; attn-small (0.04×) and attn-long (4.0×) sit clearly on either side. This is a correlation over three shapes, not an established cause.
- Template-arm models are the most accurate: p90 error 16.5% against 63.4% (text) and 74.9% (reconstruct), ordering accuracy 74.9%, and 5 of 180 configs unqualified against 12 and 26. Template authors start from the development family's program, formulation and measured history (`--knowledge F2/assets-attention-v2/template` in the launch receipts), so this difference is likely inherited from the development work, not evidence that Template authors model better.

## Fixtures and simplifications (fixture 与简化)

No code under `src/`; no `# kta:fixture` markers. Simplifications, all in the analysis:

- Action classes (read/write/check/other) come from a shell-command heuristic (`scripts/analysis/f2_common.py` `classify_command`). Counts and bytes describe composition, not time (D78).
- The retry span (lower bound) and start-to-fallback stall (upper bound) are derived from stderr warning timestamps; the true stall lies between them.
- Session start is the mtime of `provider-command.json`. Its offset from `provider-completion.json` mtime matches the recorded `seconds` (checked on `attn-medium-template-rep0-v7-round01`: 18:32:15 to 18:39:41 against 446.08 s), but mtimes are not a designed clock.
- "Controller wait" is a residual, not a measured queue time.

## Known issues (已知问题)

- Within-session time cannot be split into reading, writing and debugging with these data (ephemeral sessions, no event timestamps). Section (a) reports composition instead.
- Small samples: 3 replicates per task × arm; medians and differences in A3 are descriptive and not significance-tested.
- attn-small v7 is a continuation with v4/v6 history and different author concurrency (3 instead of 6, [E] `author_parallelism_limit`); it is not pooled with medium/long.
- Probes are chosen by the authors, not sampled, so B1 ordering statistics describe the measured neighbourhood of each model point, not the whole domain.
- These numbers are not KTA store records (no store exists before WP1); their evidence is the raw file paths and the SHA-256 manifest.
- Decision IDs D77/D78 were free on all local branches on 2026-10-30; renumber at merge if a sibling WP took them.

## Needed changes (需要的变更)

No spec change in this WP. Proposals for other WPs and for user review, each backed by the numbers above:

1. Agent concurrency and budgets (D27, D28, D35; WP6/WP8). Author time is about 79% of fresh end-to-end wall and GPU worker active time 4.1% (A1). At this scale throughput is bound by agent sessions, not the GPU; default instance counts and process quotas could be revisited before M2.
2. Codex adapter (WP8). Every F2 session started with a reconnect chain costing 13.5–19.3% of author wall. The adapter should detect this stall, record it as its own span, and choose a transport that does not stall; WP8 needs to verify which Codex setting achieves that (not checked here).
3. Session timestamps (conventions §7.1, D69, `spec/modules/agents.md` `parse_session`). F2's ephemeral sessions are why section (a) cannot split time inside a session. Proposed WP8 acceptance item: `parse_session` returns per-event timestamps, so read/write/check time can be computed.
4. Per-call timeout (D33). The 600 s cap ended sessions that then counted as lost work: 10.1% of fresh end-to-end wall (A1). Resuming the session after a timeout, rather than discarding it, would recover part of this.
5. Performance model target (D22, docs/design/50 §7). Pre-feedback models over-predict by ~17%, and resolved-pair ordering is 56–68% (B1). The model point is rarely the measured best, though regret is small. This supports measuring a top-k set per round rather than trusting top-1, and treating the L2 capacity boundary as a separate regime (B5).
6. Measurement resolution (D21; WP3). Capture A/B deviation is median 1.1%, p90 4.3%. Differences below about 3% were not resolvable in F2 batches; WP3's noise probe should confirm the threshold.
7. Context delivery (D34, D58). Template authors read 45–69 KB of knowledge and make 14–20 reads before their first write (A4). Card-based, on-demand retrieval as specified should reduce this; worth measuring once WP8 runs.
