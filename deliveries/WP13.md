### (a) Where construction time goes

Capability: per-stage and per-trajectory time accounting of F2 agent construction from raw ledgers, receipts and session files, and the Template-vs-Text comparison. Real-run evidence: the F2 runs themselves (GB10 worker, Codex `gpt-6-astra`) [S1]; this WP added no new run.

**Within-session split is not measurable.** The F2 author sessions ran as `codex exec --ephemeral` (wrapper `codex-isolated` in [S5]), and `provider.stdout` events carry no timestamps. Time inside a session therefore cannot be divided into reading context, writing code and debugging.

What is measurable: total author wall per session, provider reconnect stalls (stderr timestamps), and the composition of actions (commands, bytes, tokens). The sessions ran on the macOS control host without a GPU (workspace under `/private/tmp/agentic-f2-authors-20261027/`, see `provider-command.json` in [S5]); solving (spark) and compile and measurement (GB10 worker) happen after each session, as separate stages.

Reading B1–B5:

- Before any feedback on the task (fresh round 1), the models over-predict by a median 16.9% and only 38.1% of points fall within 10%. [S1]
- After one or more feedback rounds the median error drops to 4.1% (4.6% on unseen points), but the tail stays wide (p90 49.8%). [S1]
- Ranking is weak. On measured pairs that differ by more than 3%, the model orders 67.6% correctly overall and 55.9% in round 1. [S1]
- The frozen model point is the measured best of its stage in 25.0% of stages. Its regret against the best measured point is small (median 1.5%, p90 10.1%), because the measured set clusters around the model point. [S1]
- attn-medium is hardest: median error 11.1%, and its final recommendations under-predict by a median 14.5%. [S1] [S2]
- Its K+V working set is 1.0002 × `hw_l2_bytes`, at the L2 capacity boundary; attn-small (0.0417 ×) and attn-long (4.0002 ×) sit clearly on either side. This is a correlation over three shapes, not an established cause. [S1] [S3]
- Template-arm models are the most accurate: p90 error 16.5% against 63.4% (text) and 74.9% (reconstruct), ordering accuracy 74.9%, and 5 of 180 configs unqualified against 12 and 26. [S1]
- Template authors start from the development family's program, formulation and measured history (`--knowledge` = [S9] in the launch receipts), so the difference is likely inherited from the development work, not evidence that Template authors model better.

## Fixtures and simplifications

No code under `src/`; no `# kta:fixture` markers. Simplifications, all in the analysis:

- Action classes (read/write/check/other) come from a shell-command heuristic (`scripts/analysis/f2_common.py` `classify_command`). Counts and bytes describe composition, not time (D121).
- The retry span (lower bound) and start-to-fallback stall (upper bound) are derived from stderr warning timestamps; the true stall lies between them.
- Session start is the mtime of `provider-command.json`. Its offset to the `provider-completion.json` mtime matches the recorded duration (checked on one stage: 18:32:15 to 18:39:41 against 446.08 s recorded) [S5], but mtimes are not a designed clock.
- "Controller wait" is a residual, not a measured queue time.

## Known issues

- Within-session time cannot be split into reading, writing and debugging with these data (ephemeral sessions, no event timestamps). Section (a) reports composition instead.
- Small samples: 3 replicates per task × arm; medians and differences in A3 are descriptive and not significance-tested.
- attn-small v7 is a continuation with v4/v6 history and a different author concurrency limit (3 instead of 6, `author_parallelism_limit` in [S2]); it is not pooled with medium/long.
- Probes are chosen by the authors, not sampled, so B1 ordering statistics describe the measured neighbourhood of each model point, not the whole domain.
- These numbers are not KTA store records (no store exists before WP1); their evidence is the raw file paths and the SHA-256 manifest.
- `deliveries/evidence.schema.json` is not on this branch yet; `WP13.evidence.json` follows the field list given by the coordinator and will be re-validated after the WP0 merge.

## Needed changes

No spec change in this WP. Proposals for other WPs and for user review, each backed by the numbers above:

1. Agent concurrency and budgets (D27, D28, D35; WP6/WP8). Author time is about 79% of fresh end-to-end wall and GPU worker active time 4.1% (A1). At this scale throughput is bound by agent sessions, not the GPU; default instance counts and process quotas could be revisited before M2. [S1] [S2]
2. Codex adapter (WP8). Every F2 session started with a reconnect chain costing 13.5–19.3% of author wall. The adapter should detect this stall, record it as its own span, and choose a transport that does not stall; WP8 needs to verify which Codex setting achieves that (not checked here). [S1]
3. Session timestamps (conventions section "调用 AI 模型", D69, `parse_session` in `spec/modules/agents.md`). F2's ephemeral sessions are why section (a) cannot split time inside a session. Proposed WP8 acceptance item: `parse_session` returns per-event timestamps, so read/write/check time can be computed.
4. Per-call timeout (D33). The 600 s cap ended sessions that then counted as lost work: 10.1% of fresh end-to-end wall (A1). Resuming the session after a timeout, rather than discarding it, would recover part of this. [S1] [S2] [S8]
5. Performance model target (D22, docs/design/50 §7). Pre-feedback models over-predict by a median 16.9%, and resolved-pair ordering is 55.9–67.6% (B1). The model point is rarely the measured best, though regret is small. This supports measuring a top-k set per round rather than trusting top-1, and treating the L2 capacity boundary as a separate regime (B5). [S1] [S3]
6. Measurement resolution (D21; WP3). Capture A/B deviation is median 1.1%, p90 4.3%. Differences below about 3% were not resolvable in F2 batches; WP3's noise probe should confirm the threshold. [S1]
